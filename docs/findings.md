# Findings

Generated by `src/report.py` from `docs/findings.json` (the output of the named queries in
`sql/findings.sql`). Full result tables: [findings_results.md](findings_results.md).

Three separate subject areas, three separate organisations. They are never joined:

| Area | Source | Real or not |
|---|---|---|
| Headcount and attrition | IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance | **Fictional** dataset created by IBM data scientists |
| Absence | UCI Absenteeism at work (id 445) | Real records, courier company in Brazil, Jul 2007 to Jul 2010 |
| Time to hire | `data/simulated/SIMULATED_*.csv` | **SIMULATED** by `src/simulate_hiring.py` |

## 1. Headcount and attrition (IBM, fictional)

**1.1 One in six employees left.** 1,470 employees in the snapshot, 237 with
Attrition = Yes: an attrition rate of **16.1%**. 1,233 are still active.
The dataset is one undated snapshot, so this is a rate for the period the snapshot covers,
not an annual rate. *(f1_workforce_overall)*

**1.2 Sales loses the most people.** Sales 20.6% (92 of
446), Human Resources 19.0%, Research & Development
13.8%. By role, **Sales Representatives** leave at
**39.8%** (33 of 83), followed by Laboratory Technicians
(23.9%) and Human Resources staff (23.1%). Research Directors
have the lowest rate (2.5%). *(f2_by_department, f4_by_job_role)*

**1.3 Overtime is the clearest single split.** Employees who work overtime left at
**30.5%**, against 10.4% for those who do not
(127 of 416 vs 110 of 1,054). *(f3_by_overtime)*

**1.4 Low pay plus overtime is the riskiest group.** Overtime workers earning under 3k a month left
at **56.1%** (64 of 114). Employees with no overtime and
10k+ a month left at 6.6%. Overtime raises attrition in every income band.
The dataset gives no currency for monthly income. *(f10_overtime_x_income)*

**1.5 The first two years are the riskiest.** 29.8% of employees with 0 to 2 years
at the company left, against 8.1% at 11+ years. Leavers had an average tenure of
5.1 years, active employees 7.4. By age: 39.2%
of 18 to 24 year olds left, 10.1% of 35 to 44 year olds. By job level: level 1
26.3%, level 2 9.7%. *(f5_by_tenure_band, f8_by_age_band,
f11_by_job_level)*

**1.6 Pay, travel and satisfaction.** Attrition is 28.6% under 3k a month and
8.9% at 10k+. Frequent travellers: 24.9% vs
8.0% for non-travellers. Low job satisfaction: 22.8% vs
11.3% at very high. The median monthly income of leavers is
3,202, of active employees 5,204. *(f6, f7, f9, f1)*

These are descriptive splits of a fictional dataset. Tenure, age, job level and income move
together, so the splits overlap and do not show cause.

## 2. Absence (UCI, real records, different company)

**2.1 Total absence.** 740 absence records for 36 employees, **5,124
hours** in total. That is 142.3 hours per employee over the roughly three years
(about **47.4 hours per employee per year**). The average absence lasts
7.4 hours, the median 3 hours: most absences are short,
a few are long. *(f20_absence_overall)*

**2.2 Musculoskeletal problems and injuries cost the most hours.** Musculoskeletal and connective tissue
(16.4% of hours) and injury, poisoning (14.2%) together account for
**30.7%** of all absence hours. The most frequent reason, medical consultation
(149 records), is short: 2.8 hours on average. Non-ICD reasons (consultations,
follow-ups, physiotherapy, unjustified absence and so on) are 30.9% of hours.
*(f21_absence_by_reason)*

**2.3 Mondays are the worst day.** Monday has **29.1%** of absence hours
(1,489 h), Thursday 10.8% (553 h). Monday
absences are also longer: 9.2 hours on average vs 4.4 on Thursday.
*(f22_absence_by_weekday)*

**2.4 Season.** Autumn (Mar-May) has the most hours (32.1%), Summer (Dec-Feb)
the fewest (18.1%). Seasons are derived from the month (Southern Hemisphere), because
UCI's season code does not match the months in the file. *(f23_absence_by_season)*

**2.5 A few people account for much of the absence.** The 5 employees with the most hours have
**41.0%** of all hours. 12 of 36 employees
have an annualised Bradford factor of 250 or more (median 49). The Bradford factor
weights frequent short absences heavily; it is used here as a descriptive measure only.
*(f25_bradford, f25b_bradford_top10)*

**2.6 Data quirk.** The 40 records flagged as disciplinary failure all have
0 absence hours, and most have reason code 0. They are kept but add no hours.
*(f26_disciplinary)*

## 3. Time to hire (SIMULATED)

Everything in this section comes from a **SIMULATED** pipeline. The numbers mostly reflect the
assumptions in `src/simulate_hiring.py` (pass rates, delays, acceptance rates). They show how
the metrics are built, not how any real company hires.

**3.1 Volume.** 300 requisitions opened Jan 2025 to Jun 2026, weighted by where IBM's leavers
were. On the 30 Sep 2026 snapshot 257 are filled, 39 open, 4 cancelled.
12,109 applicants. *(f30_hiring_overall_SIMULATED)*

**3.2 Speed.** Median **time to hire 33 days** (hired candidate's application to
offer acceptance; average 32.7). Median **time to fill 37 days**
(requisition opened to offer acceptance; average 37.6). The departments are within about a
day of each other (Research & Development 32, Sales 33, Human Resources 33.5 days median time
to hire), as expected because the simulation uses the same delays for every department.
*(f33_by_department_SIMULATED)*

**3.3 Funnel.** 33.14% of applicants pass screening,
19.89% reach a first interview, 2.73% get an offer and
2.12% are hired. **Offer acceptance: 77.6%.**
*(f31_funnel_SIMULATED)*

**3.4 Source.** Employee referral gives the most hires per 100 applicants (3.99),
Job board the fewest (1.36). This follows directly from the simulation's
assumed pass rates. *(f32_by_source_SIMULATED)*
